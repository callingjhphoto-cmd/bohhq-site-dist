Keep your POS. See the whole business.

Six places, one screen. Your POS, schedule, bookings and accounts stay as they are. Back of House works beside them, reads what they hold every night, and puts it on one screen with the working one tap away.

Know tonight before doors open.

Tonight against the same weekday last year: what is on the book, who is on, what it should take and the labor that makes. It updates through service from the POS and the clock.

  • Bookings checked against the schedule and the stock for each shift.
  • Labor against the target you set, before the first guest arrives.
  • During service nothing reaches you unless it cannot wait.

A schedule that fits the night.

Hours against the covers you will actually do, labor against the target you set, and cover found when someone calls in sick. Pay is worked out for you to approve; your payroll provider still files it.

  • Staff see their own shifts and clock in and out on their own phone.
  • Timesheets, pay run and tips wait for a signature.
  • Each role sees only its own screens. Staff see their own hours, nobody else’s.

Stock that doesn’t walk.

Managers count stock by photo, video or voice and confirm each line on screen. Every invoice is checked against what was delivered and what the supplier’s own copy says.

  • Lines using more than the POS says are flagged, with the weeks behind them.
  • Supplier prices that creep up are caught on the invoice, not at year end.
  • Unsure photos wait for a person. Nothing is booked from a guess.

No surprises at month end.

Every night’s sales checked against card and cash. The week’s P&L, thirteen weeks of cash ahead against your floor, and the money brands owe you, in one place.

  • Card fees, supplier prices and dishes priced under your margin, put in dollars.
  • Payments you did not approve are flagged.
  • Every figure opens to the line behind it.

One inbox, sorted by rule.

Your mail is filed into folders by rule, with a draft ready wherever a reply is owed. Each message shows which rule sorted it and why.

  • Bookings, supplier invoices, legal, complaints and staff, each in its own folder.
  • Inspection and license mail goes into its own diary.
  • Nothing is sent without a person.

The dates that bite.

Every contract, lease and license is listed with its dates, soonest first. License dates, food-safety checks and local rules are handled before they become a problem.

  • The next date that bites, and what to do before it.
  • A compliance diary for visits and deadlines, apart from the bookings.
  • Documents on file, with what each one says and when it bites.

Questions owners ask.

Do I have to change my POS?

No. Your POS, schedule, bookings and accounts stay as they are. Back of House works beside them and puts what they hold on one screen.

Can it change anything in my systems?

No. It only reads. Links to your accounts, POS and card payments can read your figures and cannot change a thing in them.

Who checks the findings?

A person at Back of House checks every line before it reaches you. Then you mark each one right, wrong or already knew.

Does anything get sent without me?

No. No message goes to a guest, a supplier or your staff until a named owner or manager says send, for that exact message.

What does it cost?

The audit is $335 plus any applicable tax, and only if we find you $335. After that, $134 a location a month, plus any applicable tax. Both are indicative, converted from the UK price.

How do I stop?

30 days’ notice, by one message. You keep your reports and an export of your records, free.

What do I send for the audit?

Downloads from your POS, schedule, accounts and bank. On the call you get a one-page list of exactly what to download for the systems you run.

Can my staff use it?

Yes. Staff see their own shifts and hours on their own phone and clock in and out there. They see nobody else’s.

Who sees my data?

You and the people you name. It is kept apart from every other venue and never sold or shared with suppliers, drinks brands or lenders.

Is it live with venues yet?

Not yet. It is built and tested on simulated venues, and we are looking for the first three.
