Cookie policy

Back of House Drinks Ltd · for bohhq.io, bohhq.io/es/ and the Back of House app · Draft 1.2, 4 November 2026.

A cookie is a small file a website saves in your browser. "Local storage" and "session storage" are similar stores in your browser. The law treats them the same way. A site needs your consent to store or read them, unless they are strictly necessary for a service you asked for, or fall within another exception the law sets out.

The law. UK: the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003, regulation 6 and Schedule A1, as changed by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 from 5 February 2026 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/6). Spain: Ley 34/2002 (LSSI-CE), article 22.2 (https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758), read with the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos' Guía sobre el uso de las cookies (updated May 2024, https://www.aepd.es/guias/guia-cookies.pdf).

The website: bohhq.io and bohhq.io/es/

No cookies. No local storage. No analytics. No advertising or tracking. No third-party scripts or fonts. The site loads only its own pages, style sheets, scripts and fonts (checked 4 Nov 2026).

The "Sign in" and "Request a demo" buttons take you to demo.bohhq.io, which is part of the app (below).

The company that hosts the website logs your IP address for security. That is not a cookie. Our privacy notice explains it.

If you add the app to your phone's home screen, your browser keeps a copy of the app's own pages, styles, scripts, fonts and icons so it opens quickly. It never keeps venue data, sign-in pages or files.

Do we need a consent banner?

Not while everything above stays true.

  • The sign-in cookies are strictly necessary for the service you asked for (UK: PECR Schedule A1, paragraph 4, whose examples include "automatically authenticating the identity of the subscriber or user"; Spain: LSSI art. 22.2, technical storage "estrictamente necesario").
  • The language and display choices are settings you make yourself. In the UK they fall within the exception for website appearance and function preferences (Schedule A1), which asks us to tell you clearly (this page) and let you object for free: change the setting, or clear your browser's storage, and the app goes back to its defaults. In Spain, the AEPD guide treats preference cookies the user chooses, such as language, as exempt.

We review this whenever we change the website or the app. If we ever add analytics or anything else that is not exempt, we switch on a consent banner first:

  • nothing optional runs until you choose;
  • "Accept" and "Reject" in the same first layer, at the same level and with the same look (AEPD guide, May 2024);
  • no boxes ticked for you, and no "cookie wall";
  • a link to this page listing each optional cookie, its purpose, who sets it and how long it lasts;
  • a "Cookie settings" link in every footer, so you can change your mind at any time;
  • your choice kept for no more than 24 months before we ask again (the AEPD guide's good practice).
